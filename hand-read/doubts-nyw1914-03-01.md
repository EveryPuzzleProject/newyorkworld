Puzzle: Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_03_01_82.png. Solution: Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_03_08_85.png (no heading; the caption beside it reads "Herewith is shown the solution to last week's cross word puzzle").
All 32 answers run between their end squares letter for letter with the printed key.

## Doubts and oddities

1. **19-20. To declare in card playing. ~ WELD** and **11-19. A don. ~ DOW**: the key clearly prints W in r8c2. At full zoom its slanted strokes do not match the key's M (as in MELBA), so it is a real W, not a turned M. The clues want MELD and DOM (the Portuguese "don"), so the key is wrong in that one square. The grid follows the key as printed.
2. **19-20**: printed "card play-/ing" with a line-break hyphen, so the text is "card playing" (no hyphen between card and playing).
3. Damaged type, read as the words they plainly are: 24-31 "Bonds." (the B is broken, OCR ":onds"), 27-28 "noisy" (the s is broken), 5-27 (printed "5;27"), 13-14 "prickly".
4. **7-23**: printed "7-23 A card game." with no period after the pair, unlike the other clues. The clue text is unaffected.
5. The scan reads e as o and c in places ("gamo", "faco", "fover"). These are written as e.
6. Solution caption, transcribed as printed: "an dclews" (for "and clews"), and "cross work puzzle" (for "cross word").
7. Number 11: printed only in the shaded centre block ("No 11"). Title: from the heading "FUN'S CROSS-WORD PUZZLES"; the drawn words "Can You Solve Them?" are recorded as lines.
8. Many of the clue boxes are drawn by eye ("how": "eye"), because the OCR on this page splits and merges the two clue columns. The boxes also cite the OCR fragments that fall inside them.
