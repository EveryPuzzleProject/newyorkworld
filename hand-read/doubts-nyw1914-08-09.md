# Doubts: nyw1914-08-09 (No. 31)

Puzzle `Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_08_09_83.png`; solution `Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_08_16_82.png`.

1. **r1c6 K (KOM / KARA).** The letter's upper arm is blotted, so it could pass for an N. KARA (the Kara Sea, 1-4) makes K right; the confidence is moderate.
2. **9-11 "A city in northern Africa." gives YAT.** The solution prints Y-A-T in column 1, and every letter is clear. YAT is not a known city. The Y is shared with YEZD and the T with TWIN, so either the constructor forced it or something is misprinted. I record it as printed.
3. **16-18 "A mountain in India." gives SER.** The solution prints S-E-R in column 13, all clear, shared with REUS and GYOR. It is not a recognisable Indian mountain. Recorded as printed.
4. **5-22 "A city in Germany." gives HAARLEM.** Column 7 reads H-A-A-R-L-E-M, all clear. Haarlem is in the Netherlands, so the clue or the answer is wrong in the original. Recorded as printed.
5. **11-12 "A waterfall in Idaho."** In the scan the final mark after "Ida-ho" looks like a colon or a smudged period. I wrote a period. The answer TWIN is presumably meant as Twin Falls.
6. **17-18 GYOR** is printed without the accent (Győr), as the solution shows.
7. **Blocks.** The puzzle's checkered squares (and the solid or mottled squares in the solution) are written as `#`, true blocks inside the cross outline. Nothing outside the cross is printed, and that is `_`. If gxd treats decorative shaded areas as `_` (as for No. 2's 1914 centre), these could be `_` instead; they do separate words like ordinary blocks.
8. **Title and number.** "SOME GEOGRAPHY!" is the article's headline, printed for this puzzle alone (FUN's banner isn't on it). I used it as the Title in title case; drop it if headlines don't count. No number is printed, and Number is omitted. No. 31 is inferred from the next week's text, which calls No. 32 "the thirty-second in FUN'S series".
9. **Author.** "E. W. Fuller" comes from the text: "a contribution to FUN'S cross-word collection from E. W. Fuller of West Forty-sixth street". It is a contribution credit, not a byline. The Sentinel's print of "Forty-sixth" is damaged ("Forty-·ixth"); I read it as sixth.
10. **Clue punctuation.** The clues print "1 to 2—A city in Persia." and so on. They are written in the gxd form "1-2." The em dashes inside clues are kept: "A sea—Russian empire.", "An island—Hawaiian.". The words hyphenated across lines (em-pire, Hawaii-an, Ida-ho, mon-archy, Dan-ube) are joined.
11. **OCR ids dropped from boxes.json.** These OCR lines exist in the .tsv files but are left out of the citations: o44, o45, o51, o54, o55, o62 and o72 on the puzzle page, and o194 on the solution page. draw.py can't see them because it reads the .tsv with csv's default quoting, and an OCR text that begins with `"` (o50 on the puzzle page, for one) swallows the lines after it. The boxes for those lines are given by eye, so nothing is unplaced.
