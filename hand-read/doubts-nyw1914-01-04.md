# Doubts: No. 3 (World 1914-01-04), nyw1914-01-04.xd

Puzzle: Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_01_04_80_fun.png. No solution was printed (puzzles.tsv has none; the Sentinel of 1914-01-11 is not in the scans).

1. **No letters.** With no solution, every square of the puzzle is written `.` and no clue has an answer (`a-b. Clue text.` with no ` ~ `). The grid's shape is read from the printed squares: a 13-wide diamond, the same outline as No. 1, with a black cross in the centre (r5c7, r6c6-8, r7c5-9, r8c6-8, r9c7) written `_` like a shaded centre block. The cross is solid black and does separate words, so `#` would also be defensible.
2. **"6 23 An evergreen tree."** 6 is r4c10; 23 is r9c3, not in line with it. The word down from 6 runs 7 squares to 28 (r10c10). The 23 is clear at 4x (the 3 is not a broken 8). Kept as printed.
3. **"10-16 Business transactions."** 10 is r5c11; 16 is r7c4. The word down from 10 runs 5 squares to 26 (r9c11). The 1 of 16 is clear. Kept as printed.
4. **Centre lettering.** The black cross is lettered in white on a slant: "READS BOTH WAYS" is clear; above it is "I T 'S" or similar, with the first glyph blotted ("IT'S" is the reading, but "IT" with a stray mark is possible). What "reads both ways" refers to is not said; with no solution it can't be checked.
5. **Misprints kept.** "To runaway." (one word, as printed). "3-12 An ornament" has no period. "A man servant." is printed "servpnt" with a broken a. "17 18" is printed with a space for the hyphen.
6. **Title.** The heading is in big display letters, "FIND THE MISSING" over "CROSS WORDS", flanking the top square; written "Find the Missing Cross Words". The intro calls it one of "FUN'S word-cross puzzles". No number and no credit are printed.
7. **Clue order.** Left column (1-2 ... 31-32) then right column (1-8 ... 35-36), as printed. 32 clues.
