# No. 2 (World 1913-12-28): Sentinel against gxd

Sources: puzzle `Milwaukee_Sentinel_1913_12_28_77.png`; solution `Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_01_04_81_key.png`; gxd `nyworld/1913/nyw1913-12-28.xd`.

## Grid letters
The two grids are the same. The Sentinel's solution reads, row by row: Y / NEW / BOARS / TARRING / GRID_TALK / PIEL___GIRD / GIRL_____SARD / ELLS___STAY / SILO_REEL / SIXTEEN / TERNS / NIT / P. That matches gxd letter for letter. The template (shape and the shaded "1914" centre) and the given letters Y, NEW, A, R also match gxd's Numbering section. The square positions of all 32 printed numbers match too.

## Clue numbers
The left column (1-2 through 30-31) matches gxd. Five clues in the right column print different numbers. gxd has the numbers that fit the grid; the Sentinel's look like misprints, whether the World's or the Sentinel's.

| Clue | Sentinel prints | gxd |
|---|---|---|
| Young women (GIRLS) | 5 24 | 5-21 |
| Good to eat (PIE) | 2-17 | 9-17 |
| To cut lengthwise (SLIT) | 13 23 | 18-28 |
| Torn (RENT) | 28-31 | 23-31 |
| Perceives (SEES) | 9 29 | 19-29 |

The others (1-10, 3-25, N 6, 22-30, 26-32, W 7, 2-11, 4-27, 8-24, 12 20) match. "9 29" could be "19 29" with the 1 lost, but nothing of it shows at 5x zoom.

On the number separators: the Sentinel prints some pairs with a hyphen and some with only a space ("1 2", "5 6", "9 10", "N 6", "W 7", "12 20"...). This looks like a weak hyphen in the print, not a difference.

## Clue text and punctuation
- **Trailing periods.** gxd has no clue-final period on any clue. The Sentinel ends every clue with a period except two: "An iron wedge" (9-10) and "A young woman" (13-14), where none is visible.
- **N-6.** The Sentinel prints "The German for north." gxd has "German for North". gxd drops "The" and capitalises "North".
- All other clue wording matches gxd: Wild animals, Coating with tar, A grating, Conversation, An iron wedge, To bind, A young woman, Used as a gem, Ninety inches, To remain, A food chamber, To stagger, A number, Gull-like birds, The egg of an insect, Security for release, A lattice, Young women, Good to eat, To cut lengthwise, Cattle, A journey, A summons, Torn, A hidden obstacle, Perceives, To sparkle, A Hottentot's home, Not wet.
- **Clue order.** The Sentinel's two columns, read left then right, give the same order as gxd.

## Header text
- **Title.** gxd has "FUN'S Word-Cross Puzzle". The Sentinel's puzzle is headed "FUN'S NEW YEAR PUZZLE", with "FIND THE MISSING WORD!" under it. The text calls it "FUN'S word-cross puzzle" only for the previous week's puzzle. The solution page calls it "the New Year cross-word puzzle".
- **Author.** gxd has "Arthur Wynne". The Sentinel prints no credit on either page.
- **Notes.** gxd says the centre squares are shaded and printed with 1914. That agrees with the Sentinel.
- **Number.** gxd has no Number header. The Sentinel prints no number on the puzzle.
- **Garbled intro.** The Sentinel's intro is garbled. It prints "The great interest shown ii FUN'S word-cross puzzle, published last week, has prompted the puzzle editor to submit another of the same kind." Then it repeats "The great interest shown in FUN'S word-cross puzzle, pub-" and runs straight into "which agree with the definitions given below. The solution to the word-cross puzzle, given on the preceding page, shows how the squares are to be filled in, using one square for each letter." A line is evidently lost, a Sentinel typesetting fault. This is not in gxd and does not affect the puzzle.
