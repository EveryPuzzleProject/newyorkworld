# Doubts: No. 2 (World 1913-12-28), nyw1913-12-28.xd

Puzzle: Milwaukee_Sentinel_1913_12_28_77.png. Solution: Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_01_04_81_key.png. Read fresh; agrees in every point with the earlier check (no2-check.md).

## Readings and oddities
1. **Five misprinted clue pairs, kept as printed.** The type is clear in each (checked at 4x):
   - "5 24 Young women." GIRLS runs 5-21.
   - "2-17 Good to eat." PIE runs 9-17.
   - "13 23 To cut lengthwise." SLIT runs 18-28.
   - "28-31 Torn." (the 8 is the S-shaped 8 of this font, as in "S-24") RENT runs 23-31.
   - "9 29 Perceives." SEES runs 19-29. A lost 1 is possible, but the number is right-aligned like the other one-digit numbers in the column, and nothing of a 1 shows.
2. **Two clues without a period.** "9 10 An iron wedge" and "13 14 A young woman" show no period, in clean print. Written without one.
3. **Number separators.** The Sentinel prints some pairs with a hyphen and some with a space ("1 2", "5 6", "9 10", "N 6", "W 7", "12 20"...); a weak hyphen. All written in the xd's form "a-b.".
4. **Garbled intro.** The intro repeats its first sentence ("ii" for "in" the first time) and loses the line that should follow "pub-". A Sentinel typesetting fault; recorded in Notes and in the boxes file as printed.
5. **Givens.** Y (r1c7), N E W (r2c6-8), A (r3c7) and R (r4c7) are printed in the puzzle's squares; the solution prints them bold and agrees with each.
6. **"Ninety inches." for ELLS.** An English ell is 45 inches, so two ells are ninety; the clue is as printed.

## Differences from gxd's nyworld/1913/nyw1913-12-28.xd
- **Title.** gxd: "FUN'S Word-Cross Puzzle". The Sentinel's heading: "FUN'S NEW YEAR PUZZLE" (xd: "FUN'S New Year Puzzle"), subheaded "FIND THE MISSING WORD!". The intro calls the previous week's puzzle "FUN'S word-cross puzzle"; the solution page calls this one "the New Year cross-word puzzle".
- **Author.** gxd: Arthur Wynne. No credit is printed on either Sentinel page; the xd has no Author.
- **Notes.** gxd: "The center squares are shaded and printed with 1914." The xd's Notes say the same, plus the givens, subheading and intro.
- **Grid and Numbering.** Identical: every letter, the shaded centre, the givens and all 32 number positions.
- **Clue numbers.** gxd has 5-21, 9-17, 18-28, 23-31, 19-29 where the Sentinel prints 5-24, 2-17, 13-23, 28-31, 9-29 (item 1). All other pairs match.
- **N-6.** The Sentinel prints "The German for north."; gxd has "German for North".
- **Periods.** gxd has no clue-final periods; the Sentinel ends every clue with a period except 9-10 and 13-14 (item 2).
- **Order and wording.** Otherwise the same words in the same order.
