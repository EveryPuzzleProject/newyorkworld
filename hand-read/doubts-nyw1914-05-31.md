# Doubts: No. 22 (World 1914-05-31), nyw1914-05-31.xd

Puzzle page Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_05_31_90.png. The solution is on Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_06_07_85.png ("SOLUTION TO LAST WEEK'S PUZZLE", captioned "solution to cross-word puzzle No. 22"). The "SOLUTIONS TO LAST WEEK'S PUZZLES" box on the puzzle's own page belongs to another puzzle (the Mexico map) and isn't used. All 58 answers run between their end squares, letter for letter with the key, and no word in the grid is left without a clue.

1. **Number.** The heading box prints "No 22" and the next week's caption says "No. 22", but the intro says "Here is number twenty-one in FUN'S series". The xd has Number: 22 and notes the intro's wording.
2. **Shaded squares as blocks.** Inside the hourglass the X-shaped bands are printed as stippled (shaded) squares, not solid black. They separate words exactly as black squares would, so the xd writes them as `#`. The half-squares cut by the slanting sides belong to the outside and are `_`. If shading should be `_` as for a centre block, every `#` here becomes `_`.
3. **Printed pair numbers misread because of the type** (3 looks like 8, 6 like 8, and several are blotted). The page appears to read:
   - "1-8" (1-2)
   - "8-4" (3-4)
   - "5864" (58-64, hyphen missing)
   - "48-63" (43-68)

   - "11-28" (11-26: 11 to 28 isn't a line)

   The xd has the numbers in parentheses, each the only one whose end squares hold the answer. Weakest: "A common grain." as 62-18 (OAT) and "To fasten." as 42-75 (TIE). Both pairs are blotted past reading, but they are the only unclued runs those clues fit.
4. **56-13 "An exclamation." gives OOF** (column 3, from BOOB, ROAM, FLAG; the key is clear). It's an odd word for 1914 but is recorded as printed.
5. **41-42 "To reveal." gives RIOT.** "Revel" is surely meant; the misprint is kept.
6. **33-34 "Used fo rhealing or soothing."** The space is misplaced ("fo rhealing" for "for healing"). It is kept as printed.
7. **17-18 "To babble." gives CLAT** (dialect for chatter), as printed.
8. **Damaged letters.** "A river in France." The i of "in" prints like an l or 1 ("ln"), and the xd has "in". "A secon[d] time." The d is faint. "A number." The second one, 66-71, is blotted at "number." "One who forms a plan." The last two words are faint and half printed. "Meaning pale." Blotted, read from context.
9. **51-52 PLOTTER, 59-72 MAIDSERVANT, 17-41 CHARTER and 14-38 GRISTLE** are long words through the shading. They are confirmed by every crossing.
10. **Numbering above 61.** This puzzle has 76 numbers. The Numbering section uses a=10 to z=35, then A=36 to Z=61, then the Greek lowercase alphabet from α=62 (ο, omicron, is 76), as its header says. Note that ο (omicron, 76) looks like a Latin o.
11. **No credit** is printed for this puzzle, so there is no Author.
