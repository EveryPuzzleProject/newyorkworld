# Doubts: No. 20 (World 1914-05-03), nyw1914-05-03.xd

1. **Clue order.** The clues are printed in three places: down the left column under the intro (1-2 to 23-24), in four short columns under the grid (25-26 to 45-46, then 47-48 to 63-64), and down the right column from the top of the page (65-66 to 62-76). The xd takes them in that order: left column, bottom band left to right, right column. Read the page top to bottom and the right column would come before the bottom band.
2. **Misprints kept.** 15-16 "It revolves arount the earth." ("around"); 49-50 "A crytogamous plant." ("cryptogamous"). 38-55 "The past perfect of tread." gives TROD (a past tense, not a past perfect) as printed.
3. **Missing periods kept.** 5-6 "A farm implement", 29-30 "Part of your face" and 8-25 "An outdoor game" fill their lines to the margin and show no period. The print is clear there, so they are written without one.
4. **Odd punctuation after the pair.** "35-42-A large cask." and "62-76-Toward." print a hyphen where the others have a space; "65;66" has a mark like a semicolon. Written in the usual form (35-42., 62-76., 65-66.).
5. **Damaged letters read in context.** 19-20 "Autumn time." (broken A); 37-38 "A little stream." (the A prints as a dash, OCR "—"); 39-46 "To reduce" (broken T); 45-52 "An affectionate" (a stray mark before "affectionate"). The letter-spaced lines (15-16 "r e v o l v e s", 23-24 "y o u  d o", 6-11 "Phy s i c a l", 17-24 "l o n g") are written with normal spacing.
6. **The compass note.** "Nos. 1, 14, 51 and 64 are the four points of the compass." ends the clue list. In the key those squares hold N (NIAS), S (THIS), E (EATS) and W (ENOW). It is in Notes and in the boxes file as a "note" line, not as a clue.
7. **Answers worth a look.** 1-2 NIAS (a young hawk; "nyas"/"eyas"), 39-40 METEYARD, 37-38 RUNLET, 67-68 BESEEMLY, 63-64 ENOW are all clearly printed in the key and recorded as printed. The S of LOSE (r6c14) and the E of ENOW (r20c17) are blotted in the key but fixed by FAST and NOTE.
8. **Numbering past Z.** There are 76 numbers. a=10..z=35 and A=36..Z=61 as nyw1914-08-02.xd does; 62-76 are the symbols $ % & * + / < > ? @ ^ { } [ ], listed in the Numbering header. No uppercase letter in this Numbering is a printed given letter.
9. **Number.** From the "No 20" panel under the centre (outside the grid); the intro also says "the twentieth in FUN'S series". Its credit is in the intro: "FUN'S puzzle solvers are indebted to Miss Elizabeth M. Butzinger of West One Hundred and Thirty-fifth street, New York City". The next week's solution text says another puzzle "by the same contributor is given on Page 2 today".
10. **Title.** The heading is split around the X: "FUN'S" at the top, "CROSS-" / "WORD" on the left, "PUZ-" / "ZLES" on the right; written "FUN'S Cross-Word Puzzles". "CAN YOU SOLVE THIS ONE?" heads the intro in the left column.
