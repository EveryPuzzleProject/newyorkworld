# Doubts: No. 4 (World 1914-01-11), nyw1914-01-11.xd

Puzzle: the Farmers Mail and Breeze's reprint, Kansas_Farmer_and_Mail_and_Breeze_1914_02_14_16.jpg (1914-02-14, p. 16). Solution: the Milwaukee Sentinel's Milwaukee_Sentinel_1914_01_18_83_key.png, whose caption gives it as the solution to "last week's cross word puzzle".

1. **15-16 "A son of Lot." = ROAB.** The Sentinel's solution clearly prints R O A B (checked at 2x). Lot's son is Moab. r7c1 is unchecked (column 1 has only this square), so nothing else in the grid confirms either letter. The xd follows the solution (R), as the earlier hand reads did for solution-vs-sense conflicts; MOAB is surely what was meant.
2. **The puzzle is a reprint.** The clue text, numbers and grid are the Kansas paper's, five weeks after the World's date. Whether it reproduces the World's wording exactly can't be checked here (the Sentinel's copy of 1914-01-11 is not in the scans). Its heading ("A Puzzle For Mail and Breeze Boys and Girls"), intro ("HERE is a cross-word puzzle... For instance, 1-2 means to help; the word is aid.") and prize notice are the Kansas paper's own; they are recorded in the boxes file, but no Title is taken from them. The centre lettering "CAN YOU SOLVE IT?" may also be the reprint's.
3. **No number, no credit** on either page.
4. **Same template as No. 3.** The grid outline, black centre cross and all 36 number positions match No. 3 (1914-01-04) exactly. Here the down pairs 6-28 and 10-26 are printed correctly; No. 3's Sentinel printing has 6-23 and 10-16 for the same squares.
5. **Clue order.** Left column 1-2 ... 31-32; right column starts with 33-34 and 35-36, then 1-8 ... 25-32. Written in that printed order.
6. **Print quality.** The Kansas clue type is small and soft but every clue is legible at 1.4x; "5-6" (OCR "6-6") is clearly 5-6 at 5x. 29-30 and 21-30 share one OCR line (o286), so each has its own box.
7. **Answers worth a glance (all as printed, all fit):** SMEE "A water bird." (a duck), OWED "An obligation.", BRED "What she was in Old Kentucky." (from the song "She Was Bred in Old Kentucky"), ASTORIA "A part of Long Island."
