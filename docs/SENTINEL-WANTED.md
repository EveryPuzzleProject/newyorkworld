# Milwaukee Sentinel pages still worth saving (2026-11-16)

The Sentinel carried the World's FUN section on FUN's own Sundays from December 1913 to 1915-09-12. Its last
crossword item is that day's solution-only page; Alex found no puzzle after it and none in 1916. So it can't
fill the fall-1915 hole (Nos. 91-100 by the count, 1915-09-19 to 11-14). That needs another paper that carried
FUN then. Those Sundays are recorded as Sentinel no-hits.

**Still open.** The Sentinel now holds every puzzle from No. 1 to No. 33 except No. 4 (No. 34 was never used), and every solution except No. 3:

| Sunday | What it adds |
|---|---|
| 1914-01-11 | No. 4 and No. 3's solution (searched, not found) |

Save as before into Downloads; Claude files them (`Milwaukee_Sentinel_<yyyy>_<mm>_<dd>_<page>.png`).

## Found since (2026-11-16, Alex)

- **1913-12-21, FUN p. 14: No. 1**, Wynne's first crossword, on the World's own day. It's the first partner copy found.
  The record's old "No. 1", Pittsburgh Press 1918-12-01, was a misread: its "1" is the grid's first cell number, and the puzzle is Henry Zarske's of 1918. Its number is withdrawn.
- **1914-11-15, FUN p. 15: No. 46**, printed with its number: Edward Thimme's two-in-one, the puzzle the Journal never ran. The page has No. 45's solution too.
- **1914-01-18 pp. 81, 83 and 01-25 pp. 67, 68**: full pages of Ben's clippings (Nos. 5 and 6, and the solutions to Nos. 4 and 5). Filed in the scans repo. They are not in `pages.tsv`, which lists the files the record points to; those remain Ben's clippings.
- **1915-02-14**: searched, nothing found (no-hit).
- **1913-12-28, FUN pp. 14-15: No. 2**, the New Year puzzle (1-2 Wild animals), and **No. 1's solution**, printed upside down.
  Both match gxd. Nos. 1 and 2 now have partner copies of puzzle and solution, on the World's own Sundays.
- **1914-01-04 pp. 80, 81**: full pages of Ben's clippings (No. 3 and No. 2's solution).
- **1914-02-08, FUN pp. 13, 15: No. 8** (Mrs. M. B. Wood), a sharper copy, and **No. 7's solution** ("Solution to No 7 Puzzle").
- **1914-05-31, FUN p. 15: No. 22.** The intro calls it "number twenty-one", FUN's own miscount. Its solutions box is the Mexico map puzzle, which confirms FUN ran no crossword on 05-24.
- **1915-01-17, FUN p. 5: No. 54's solution.** No new crossword that day.
- **1915-01-31, FUN p. 7: the U of F-U-N**, the same as the Journal's. No solution is printed: FUN promised the whole puzzle's solution for 02-14, a Sunday not found in any paper.
- **1915-02-28, FUN p. 7: the solutions to 02-21's Pentacle** and its word square.
- **Searched, nothing found:** 1914-01-11 (No. 4's page), 1915-04-04, 04-11, 05-09, 05-16, 05-23, 06-06. **1915-05-30** was a short issue with no puzzle (recorded as not carried).
- **1914-02-15 to 03-29 (Nos. 9-15), 11 pages:** puzzles for Nos. 9, 10, 12, 13, 14 and 15, and the solutions to Nos. 8, 10, 12, 13 and 14, all linked by label.
  The Sentinel names two puzzles the Journal left unnumbered: the George Washington puzzle is "the tenth" (No. 10), and the 03-15 two-in-one is captioned "No 13".
  Constructor credits the Journal lacks: Evangeline Kearney (No. 12), Harry Cleaver of Montreal (No. 13), "G. H. F." (No. 14), Florence A. Reif (No. 15).
  Not saved: No. 11's puzzle page (03-01; only the solutions page came), and the solution pages of 02-22 and 03-08.
- **1914-04-05 to 04-26 (Nos. 16-19), 8 pages:** each puzzle with the next week's labelled solution (Nos. 15-18), all linked by label, and No. 19 linked to the 05-03 "Solution to No 19".
  The Sentinel numbers No. 18: the Journal's 04-19 puzzle is unnumbered, but the Sentinel's intro calls it "the eighteenth", and its solution is labelled "No 18".
  No. 17's clue list as printed starts at 3-4 "It comes before Easter". Credits: Katherine Reilly (No. 16), Alex B. Miller of Portsmouth, Va. (No. 18), Olga Lehander (No. 19).
- **1914-06-14 to 08-16 (Nos. 23-32), 14 pages:** puzzles for Nos. 23-30 and 32, and solutions to Nos. 23 and 25-31, all linked (by label for 23, 25 and 26, by grid for the rest).
  Credits the Journal rows lack: H. J. Stack (No. 23), Ernest A. Grant (No. 24), Charles L. Rives (No. 26), W. G. H. Tiedemann (No. 27; the editor changed some of his words), Aloysius E. Pracny (No. 29, five puzzles in one, unnumbered here), T. W. Martin, Jr. (No. 30), William Callander (No. 32).
  No. 25's first clue reads 2-3 here and 1-3 in the Journal row: check when reading.
- **The stragglers (2026-11-16):** No. 11 (03-01, FUN p. 12) and the solutions to Nos. 9 (02-22), 11 (03-08), 21 (05-17, a solutions-only Sunday) and 24 (06-28, FUN p. 4), all linked.
- **1914-08-23 p. 76, 08-30 p. 84:** the solutions to No. 32 (FUN p. 7) and No. 33 ("Solution to Cross-Word Puzzle No 33", FUN p. 4, printed the week No. 35 ran). Linked.
