# How the World's FUN crosswords were printed (1914, as the Minneapolis Journal reprinted them)

- **Clues name a word by the two numbers printed in its end squares**: "1-2 To unite in
  matrimony", "3-12 Of vigorous health". There are no Across and Down lists. In the packet
  each clue has the pipeline's slot label and the printed pair beside it ("A2 (printed 1-2)").
  Keep the slot labels as the packet gives them; the printed pair is evidence. The pairs were
  read off the clue list and the grid together and can be wrong, above all for Down words.
  When a clue's text plainly belongs to another slot (the key's answer for that slot fits the
  text, and the text's own printed pair names that slot's end squares), move it: give the text
  under the slot it belongs to, with "why" saying what the key's answer is and which pair the
  sheet prints by it ("moved from A18: the key gives RIIS, printed 23-24"). The slot it came
  from then takes the text that belongs to it, or stays missing. Every move shows on the proof
  page as the text step's change with its reason; a move you are not sure of goes in "unsure"
  instead. (Decided by Alex, 2026-11-01: moving by key is allowed when the reason is shown.)
- **The grid is a template**: a diamond, a cross, an hourglass. The squares outside it and
  its shaded centre block are not squares of the puzzle (`_` in the grid). Blocks are printed
  as shading, not solid black.
- **The World's number is printed in the shaded centre block** ("No 11"), where the OCR
  cannot read it. The packet lists that block as a printed line ("No. ? (the label in the
  grid's centre block: unread)") with its crop on the sheet: read the number from the crop,
  correct the line's text to "No. 11" and give that line as the evidence for "number". If the
  crop shows no number (some weeks the centre is empty), leave the line and say so.
- **There is no title.** The Journal prints a banner ("Cross-Word Puzzles / Can You Solve
  Them?") and sometimes a number heading ("Cross-Word Puzzle No. 21"). Label the banner
  "section"; a line with the number is evidence for "number", not a title. Leave "title" null
  unless a line names this week's puzzle ("A George Washington Cross-Word Puzzle": that is a
  title).
- **No byline.** A credit line ("This cross-word puzzle is furnished by H. J. Stack of
  Hartford, Conn.") is a "credit" line, kept; it is not a byline.
- **The answers come from the following week's printed solution**, matched by the pipeline.
  Where the key's letters look wrong for a clue, say so as a key-misread finding.
- The copyright line and the clue-list instructions ("Fill in the small squares with words
  corresponding to the clues and definitions given below") are "instructions" / "credit"
  lines, kept.
