# Open research: finding is the work

Tasks where the effort is locating a page, not saving it. Leave the tab open and download
when the rate limit allows; a clip is enough to settle the question meanwhile. In order of value.

**First: when does the Globe drift off the World's date?** The Globe equals the World in 1917 but
not later: its 1920-05-02 puzzle is the one Campbell dates 1919-10-12, 29 weeks earlier. Dated
copies from the Pittsburg Press, the Journal, the LA Evening Express or the Buffalo Courier Express
across 1918 to 1923 would plot it. Evidence, what it affects and other corrections, including to
items 2 and 3 below: [docs/CAMPBELL-CROSSCHECK.md](docs/CAMPBELL-CROSSCHECK.md).

**1918 partners (five leads, unverified; observed by Alex DeJarnatt on Newspapers.com, 2026-10-31, pages not yet downloaded because of the daily limit; he will fetch them 2026-11-01).** (1) The Boston Globe's 1918-01-06 puzzle ran in the Pittsburgh Press on 1918-01-20, so Pittsburgh's two-week lag behind the Globe carries on into 1918. (2) The Shreveport Times and the New Orleans States appear to be in step with each other in January 1918. (3) Alex also observed that the Boston Globe's 1917-12-16 puzzle ran in both the Shreveport Times and the New Orleans States on 1918-01-06, a three-week lag. Contrast 1917, when New Orleans was two weeks behind the World (the 07-22 solutions box): either those two papers' lag grew by a week around the turn of the year or the Globe's own order has already shifted; the indexed pages will say which. (4) The Globe's 1917-12-23 puzzle ran in the Shreveport Times on 1918-01-20, a four-week lag, against three weeks the Sunday before (12-16 to 01-06). Addendum (Alex, same day, unverified): the Shreveport Times of 1918-01-20 prints the answer key to its 01-06 puzzle, so 01-13 carried no puzzle: a deliberate skipped week, not a reorder, and Shreveport's lag behind the Globe went from three weeks to four by skipping. The 01-06 to 01-20 link should verify as a normal continuity link once the pages are indexed; 01-13 then gets `not-carried` for Shreveport (and New Orleans if it matches). (5) Alex clipped the Pittsburgh Press 1918-02-24 FUN puzzle (`newyorkworld-scans/other/The_Pittsburgh_Press_1918_02_24_puzzle_and_solutions_clip.webp`; full page to come when his limit resets). It prints two dated solutions side by side, "Solution to Cross-Word Puzzle of Jan. 27" and "of Feb. 3", so it carries dated solutions boxes like Tacoma's 1917-07-22, and two solutions in one week. Whether those dates are the World's or Pittsburgh's own is to be settled from the pages (Pittsburgh's 01-27 and 02-03 puzzles against the Globe's). Its intro names a reader-author in World Magazine wording: Edward Reich, of South Second Street, Brooklyn, "is the author of this week's Cross-Word Puzzle. The solution and another Cross-Word Puzzle will be printed in an early number of this Magazine". So the World was running bylined reader puzzles, with addresses, by February 1918 (see also the catalog leads below). Addendum (Alex, same day, unverified): the dates on those two solutions are not Pittsburgh's own printing dates. The "Feb. 3" solution answers the puzzle Pittsburgh printed on 1918-02-17, so they are the World's dates and Pittsburgh's lag is two weeks, as in 1917. That makes the 02-24 page a dated anchor for 1918, the same kind as Tacoma 1917-07-22: World 1918-02-03 = Pittsburgh 02-17, and by the same token World 01-27 = Pittsburgh 02-10. Once the page is indexed, check whether the Globe's held 1918-02-03 page carries that same puzzle (first clue "1-6 To diminish", a 1-88 grid with a diamond of black squares). If it does, the Globe was still on the World's date in February 1918, which bounds where its drift starts. **First 1918 batch indexed (2026-11-01; 24 pages, matched by first clue, so still the indexer's reading):** the Globe was on the World's dates in Jan-Feb 1918: the one dated solution box, Pittsburgh 02-24 ('of Jan. 27', 'of Feb. 3'), answers the puzzles the Globe printed on 01-27 and 02-03. None of the five is settled until the pages are indexed. **Pittsburgh Sep-Dec 1918 (14 full pages, 2026-11-01; matched by first clue, medium):** from late summer Pittsburgh runs AHEAD of the Globe, not behind. Pittsburgh 09-01 ('1-3 A light, lively dance') is the Globe's 12-22 puzzle, 16 weeks before the Globe; its key (DOVE/DARTS/ELMS) answers the unheld 08-25 puzzle = Globe 09-29, 5 weeks before. The Globe's order floats. Every puzzle on these 14 pages matched a Globe page; Pittsburgh's only puzzles no Globe page holds are 09-08 (7-54 A jot) and 12-22 (island empire), from the autumn batch. Autumn gap (Alex browsed the issues): Pittsburgh printed no crossword on 09-22, 09-29, 10-06, 10-13 or 11-24; 09-15 carries only the 09-08 key, 10-20 and 10-27 are Liberty Loan / Stars & Stripes one-off word-crosses, the 11-03 key (STARS...) answers the 10-27 Stars & Stripes grid, and FUN returns 11-10. ## Leads for the catalog (not this run)

Sightings found along the way that belong in `catalog/publications/`, parked here so they
aren't lost; move each into a catalog entry with its source.

- **New York World: bylined reader puzzles in 1918** (for `catalog/publications/new-york-world.md`; Alex DeJarnatt via the Pittsburgh Press clip of 1918-02-24, 2026-10-31, unverified until the page is indexed). The intro credits a named reader with a Brooklyn street address as "the author of this week's Cross-Word Puzzle", in World Magazine wording, so FUN was printing reader-submitted puzzles with bylines by February 1918. A lead, not a transcription; details under "1918 partners" above.
- **Brooklyn Daily Eagle**: started a weekly Sunday crossword on 1924-05-18 (Alex DeJarnatt,
  Newspapers.com, 2026-10-30). Newspapers.com publication id 13893. Whose puzzle, and
  whether it's the World's, not yet checked.
- **Liberty** (weekly magazine, 1924-1950): printing crosswords by 1929, a potentially exciting
  series, not in the catalog yet (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-31). The 1929-06-01 issue, v6 n21,
  pp. 80-81 ([scan](https://archive.org/details/LibertyV06N2119290601/page/n79/mode/2up)) has
  two puzzles under the heading "Two New Puzzles: Take Your Choice", no credit, each with a
  small "Answer to last week's puzzle" grid. So weekly, two at a time, in that issue.
  Archive.org's `libertymagazine` collection has 104 items, the earliest 1925-07-11, the latest
  1948-10-01, thin before 1932 (1925 one, 1929 three), 22 in 1936; items carry OCR text, and
  the table of contents lists "Cross Word Puzzles", so that line finds them. Gale's Liberty
  Magazine Historical Archive 1924-1950 is the fuller run if a library has it. Bell Syndicate
  link (Wikipedia, not yet checked against a primary source): founded by Robert McCormick and
  Joseph Medill Patterson; John Neville Wheeler, Bell's founder, was its first editor, 1924 to
  early 1926, while still running Bell. Crossword Craze has Bell syndicating crosswords from
  February 1924 (see docs/CAMPBELL-CROSSCHECK.md), so whether Liberty's puzzles have any Bell
  connection is worth asking. Not yet known: when they start, who made them, how long they ran.
- **Bell Syndicate's crossword, 1924-25**: moved to its own project,
  [EveryPuzzleProject/bell](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/bell). Sightings in the
  Indianapolis Star, Burlington Free Press and Yonkers Statesman, and the open questions, are
  in its printings.tsv and RESEARCH.md.
- **George Matthew Adams Service's 1924 crossword**: The Daily Times, Davenport, Iowa,
  1924-12-29 (Alex DeJarnatt, Newspapers.com, 2026-10-31) prints "Your Cross Word Puzzle",
  "(Copyright, 1924, George Matthew Adams)", no byline. Small, 9 by 9; its footer says "The
  solution will appear tomorrow" and it prints "Solution of Yesterday's Puzzle", so daily.
  Horizontal clues are numbered 1-10 and the vertical ones go on from 11, sharing 1 and 2. This
  is seven months before the catalog's only record of the feature, E&P's July 1925 directory
  ("Puzzles, Cross Word", William Stevens, daily; census/ep-crossword-features.csv). Whether
  Stevens made this one, and when the feature starts, not yet known.

## How firm the no-hits are

The gap-Sunday sweeps of 2026-10-31 (Los Angeles Tribune, Shreveport, Pittsburgh, Tacoma) recorded a Sunday as no-hit when Alex DeJarnatt's "puzzle" and "crossword" searches downloaded no page for it. Alex says he did not work hard at the gaps, so these no-hits are "nothing turned up quickly", not proof of absence. If a wanted puzzle (see wanted.tsv) might sit on one of them, search that Sunday again, and browse the issue if the text search misses.
