# Open research: finding is the work

Tasks where the effort is locating a page, not saving it. Leave the tab open and download
when the rate limit allows; a clip is enough to settle the question meanwhile. In order of value.

1. **The Journal's puzzle that moved off its page.** The next week's solution proves a puzzle
   ran, but it isn't on the usual puzzles page: 1916-08-13 and 1916-09-10 (an all-comics page
   sat where the puzzle page should be), 1917-01-14 and 01-21 (the 01-28 solution is Botjer's
   diamond, so 01-21's puzzle exists), 1915-06-13 and 06-20 (06-27's solution isn't 06-06's).
   Browse the whole issue, not only the feature section.
   - 1917-01-14: no puzzle found (comics inserts, Clubland); 01-21's puzzle sits at the bottom of a Clubland page (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-30).
   - 1915-06-13 and 06-20: whole issues browsed, only answer keys, no puzzle; the 06-27 solution answers a puzzle on neither date. Page missing from Newspapers.com or key run without puzzle (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-30).
   - 1916-08-13, 09-10: all-comics page where the puzzle page sits; see searches.tsv.
2. **The Journal's 1917 run and its end.** The Journal still carried the puzzle on 1917-02-04
   (solution only) and ran the Globe's puzzles on the Globe's dates. Find any Journal Sunday
   after 02-04: its puzzle page may have moved again, and the heading is plain "Cross Word
   Puzzle". The last one found fixes when the Journal dropped FUN.
   **Settled (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-30):** Alex went through the issues page by page for 1917-02-04, 02-11, 02-18, 02-25 and 03-04: no puzzle on any (02-04 carries a solution only); Frank King's "Section of Fun" comics page sits where the puzzle page was, apparently its replacement. The Journal's last FUN puzzle is therefore 1917-01-28 (its solution ran 02-04); 01-21's sat in Clubland at the foot of a page.
3. **World Nos. 3 to 6 (1914-01-04 to 01-25): a community ask.** Not on Newspapers.com: a
   thorough search of January 1914 finds no partner copy before the Journal's 02-01 (Alex
   DeJarnatt, 2026-10-30). They need the World's own FUN section: Will Shortz's collection,
   the New York Public Library's microfilm, or another researcher's copy (T Campbell reprints
   Nos. 1 and 2). Anyone with access to one of those can close this gap with four pages.
4. **The 1915-01-31 mystery.** Consecutive with 01-24, yet its solution doesn't fit 01-24's
   puzzle. Look at both pages again: was a different puzzle printed elsewhere on 01-24, or is
   the 01-31 solution a repeat of an older one?
   **Settled (Alex DeJarnatt, Newspapers.com, 2026-10-30):** the three puzzles were shaped as letters, F on 01-24, U on 01-31, N on 02-07 (FUN); no solutions ran for them as far as Alex could tell, perhaps a contest. So the 01-31 solution that fits nothing is not a repeat of an older one: the F-U-N puzzles were unanswered. To confirm: what the 02-14 and 02-21 pages carry.
5. **The Globe's skipped Sunday, 1917-05-20.** Its 05-27 solution answers 05-13. Confirm that
   no puzzle ran on 05-20 anywhere in the issue.
   **Settled (Alex DeJarnatt, Newspapers.com, 2026-10-30):** searched the 05-20 issue, no puzzle found; consistent with the skipped Sunday.
6. **The Du Puy Syndicate.** Who they were and what else they syndicated: the Journal's FUN
   page credits them from 1914-02-15 to 04-12, the Press Publishing Co. from 04-19. Any other
   paper's FUN page in early 1914 with the same line, or trade-press mention, would place them.
7. **The Pittsburgh Press's first FUN puzzle, 1916-06-11: found** (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-30; page 73, no previous
   solution, no announcement; it is the Journal's 06-04 puzzle, so the Press ran a week behind the Journal from the
   start). Tacoma's first: 1916-08-27 (Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-10-30; 08-20 had none), the Journal's 08-20 puzzle one week later, with a 'last week's' solution to a puzzle it never printed (the same ARABIAN/CAYENNE solution the Journal printed 08-20).
   **Pittsburgh found (Alex DeJarnatt, Newspapers.com, 2026-10-30):** 1916-06-11, page 73, Sunday Press comic section, a page of fun and puzzles: a rebus at the top, a cross-word puzzle under it that looks like a FUN puzzle; no "FUN" or New York World credit. Page downloaded as The_Pittsburgh_Press_1916_06_11_Page_73.jpg, awaiting intake. **Tacoma (Alex DeJarnatt, Newspapers.com, 2026-10-30):** first FUN puzzle found on 1916-08-27, weekly after that; 1916-08-20 has no puzzle, though the 08-27 page carries a previous solution: the run began 08-27 with a solution for a puzzle Tacoma never printed. Scan saved by Alex, awaiting intake.
8. **Where the Journal printed its solutions when not on the puzzle page.** Several 1915 pages
   carry a solution with no puzzle or a puzzle with no solution; the issue may have split them.
